# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Wantlist is for everyday people who want one simple place to collect products from any store, share the list, and let friends reserve items without coordination friction. Users are not expected to be technical or shopping-power users; the interface must feel obvious on first contact and calm during repeated use.

## Product Purpose

Wantlist lets a person create a universal wishlist, add items from different shops, share the list by link or invite, and manage reservations privately. Success means someone can add an item, share the list, and understand reservation status without reading instructions or worrying about who can see what.

## Brand Personality

Vibrant, social, and light. The product should feel friendly and shareable, with enough color to make wishlist activity feel alive, but never so much visual energy that the UI becomes tiring. The tone is simple, direct, and approachable in Brazilian Portuguese.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS aesthetics, purple or blue-purple gradients, glassmorphism, low-contrast pastel text, and decorative effects that do not help the task. Do not make it feel corporate, heavy, or visually exhausting. The product should not look like a dashboard for experts; it should look like a tool normal people can use immediately.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next action obvious. Adding, sharing, reserving, and changing visibility must be visually clear without extra explanation.
2. Keep social energy lightweight. Use color to signal activity and delight, not to decorate every surface.
3. Protect trust. Privacy, reservation state, invitation access, and ownership actions must look precise and reliable.
4. Prefer familiar controls. Buttons, fields, links, badges, and icons should behave like users expect.
5. Let the product breathe. Screens should stay simple, readable, and easy on the eyes, especially on mobile.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and interactive controls. Support reduced motion for animated surfaces. Do not rely on color alone for reservation or visibility state; pair color with clear text labels and icons where useful. Keep touch targets comfortable and avoid dense layouts that require precision from casual mobile users.
